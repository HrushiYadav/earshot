# v0.2 comparison: earshot vs CLAP vs a trained classifier

Generated by `scripts/v02/*.py`. Apple M4 MacBook Air 16 GB, MPS backend, fp16 (AST) / fp32 (CLAP BatchNorm needs fp32). Docker quit, plugged in.

**Memory metric used throughout:** peak **MPS driver-allocated memory** (`torch.mps.driver_allocated_memory()`). This is the actual GPU memory the model + activations + KV cache uses. Process RSS is misleading on macOS because PyTorch reserves a ~9 GB MPS region up-front that the process always counts in `resident_set_size` even for `mock=1` second forward passes — so RSS reports the reservation, not the model. Model weight bytes alone are also misleading: 3–4 GB of weights expand to 9–10 GB at peak during a forward pass.

## Part 1 — ESC-50 fold 1 (400 clips × 50 classes, 5 s each)

Two CLAP prompt styles tested for fairness; earshot tested with and without contextual calibration (see below).

| method | top-1 | top-5 | median / clip | peak MPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AST + LR** (trained on folds 2–5) | **0.9050** (362/400) | 0.9950 | 128.6 ms *(batch-of-8 amortized)* | 1.20 GB |
| **CLAP** zero-shot, *readable phrases* (`"the sound of {phrase}"`) | 0.8825 (353/400) | 0.9925 | 25.7 ms | 1.05 GB |
| **CLAP** zero-shot, *raw snake_case labels* (`"the sound of a {snake}"`) | 0.8375 (335/400) | 0.9900 | 24.6 ms | 1.05 GB |
| **earshot**, raw P(Yes) argmax | 0.8225 (329/400) | 0.9725 | 4319.5 ms | 10.31 GB |
| **earshot**, per-question calibrated | **0.8500** (340/400) | 0.9700 | 4319.5 ms | 10.31 GB |

### Per-question contextual calibration (Zhao et al. 2021 style)

For each question `q`, run it on three neutral inputs and use the mean logit as the bias:

```python
bias_q = mean logit(P(Yes | neutral, "Is there the sound of X_q?"))
calibrated_score = logit(P(Yes | audio, "Is there the sound of X_q?")) - bias_q
```

Neutral inputs: `clips/silence_1.wav` (RMS 0.0016), 5 s of white noise at RMS 0.0001, `clips/room_noise_1.wav` (RMS 0.0050). The bias is uneven — the 5 most-biased-to-Yes questions on neutral audio are `wind` 0.418, `sea_waves` 0.375, `engine` 0.351, `breathing` 0.339, `clock_tick` 0.311.

## Takeaway

For fixed sound labels, use a trained classifier or CLAP: faster, lighter, more accurate. For questions about what was said or how it was said, earshot separates the classes far better (AUROC 0.93–0.999 vs ≤0.82 for CLAP's best prompt). CLAP matches sounds to descriptions; it wasn't trained to understand speech.

Specifically:
- On **50-way fine-grained sound classification** (ESC-50), a tiny classifier on AST embeddings (0.905) is the top-1 winner, and CLAP with the readable phrases (0.8825) beats earshot (0.850 calibrated). earshot pays 4.3 s/clip and 10.3 GB MPS for a result that's ~5 pp behind CLAP and ~6 pp behind AST+LR.
- On **four binary contextual/paralinguistic tasks**, earshot wins on accuracy and AUROC on every one. CLAP's similarity scores are not even consistently above random on most prompts (AUROC drops to 0.25–0.37 on the `say` and `stop` tasks — i.e. below 0.50, meaning the model is ranking the wrong class higher more often than not).

## Caveats

- **Training overlap.** CLAP (LAION-Audio-630k, AudioCaps), AST (AudioSet) and Qwen2.5-Omni all trained on web-scale audio. ESC-50, RAVDESS and Speech Commands were all released before some of the training data was collected, so duplicates or near-duplicates may exist in the training sets. **All scores may be optimistic**; transfer to truly novel sounds is unknown.
- **RAVDESS = acted emotion with two neutral sentences** ("Kids are talking by the door", "Dogs are sitting by the door"). Same actors, same words, different emotion — so the calm/angry test is a tone-only test, which is what we want. But actors reading lines, not real-world emotion.
- **Speech Commands v0.02 = single words, not sentences.** stop is a keyword-spotting task, not a contextual test. 1 s clips at 16 kHz, 250 stop + 250 mixed (50 each from go, no, yes, up, down).
- **macOS `say` is synthetic** (n=20). Useful as a sanity check that the question reaches the right answer on a tiny prosody-only signal; not a real benchmark.
- **One machine only** (M4 Air 16 GB). Latency numbers reflect this hardware.
- **0.5 threshold not tuned.** On that task, accuracy is 0.68 but AUROC is 0.93 — the rank orderings are good, the threshold just isn't calibrated for the prevalence. A tuned threshold would close most of that gap.
