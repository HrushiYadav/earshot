# earshot — Stage 6 full benchmark

Generated by `scripts/bench.py`.

- **Device:** Apple M4 MacBook Air 16 GB
- **Model:** Qwen2.5-Omni-3B (thinker-only)
- **Backend:** PyTorch MPS, eager attention
- **Precision:** fp16
- **Clip:** `calm_1.wav`, trimmed to `3.0`&nbsp;s (48000 samples @ 16000&nbsp;Hz — the live window size)
- **Warm-up:** 120&nbsp;s wall-clock with the full question set, lid open, laptop plugged in. Soaks the M4 Air into its steady-state thermal envelope.
- **Timed passes per N:** 20 (median + p90). N=32 sequential uses 10 (would take ~8&nbsp;min otherwise).

Re-run after freeing memory (Docker quit); earlier runs were slowed by swap.

Raw data: [`bench.csv`](bench.csv). Chart: [`latency.png`](latency.png).

## Live rate

`earshot.live --source calm_1.wav --no-view --log --duration 60` — real run, real worker thread, real skip-to-newest.

| passes | wall (s) | loop (s, steady-state) | pass/s | median latency (s) | p90 latency (s) |
|-------:|---------:|------------------------:|-------:|--------------------:|----------------:|
| 40 | 137.0 | 59.4 | 0.67 | 1.482 | 1.528 |

`loop` is the window from the first recorded pass's `ts` to the last pass's `ts + latency_ms` &mdash; it excludes the subprocess's own ~80&nbsp;s model-load warm-up, so the pass/s rate reflects steady-state, not the cold start.

## Notes

- The fanless M4 Air throttles under sustained load; the 2-minute warm-up before timing is what brings numbers into their steady-state. Don't skip it.
- Speedup is the stable claim; absolute latency shifts run-to-run as the MPS allocator warms up.
- Per-question rows past N=11 are simple ad-hoc bool questions so the batched suffix has rows to fill; the production YAML has 9 model bools. Adding them to the fork doesn't change the cost model &mdash; each row is a separate suffix forward.
